      Key and method
      • Hill summit, height in metres. Faded when hidden from the viewpoint.
      • Viewpoint. Tap a hill or any spot to look from there.
      • Line falls towards the arrow, 0.5 to 3% (1 in 200 to 1 in 33).
      • Steeper than 3%. Arrows point downhill, as on OS road gradients. No arrow means roughly level.

      Hills are found in terrain data by prominence: how far a summit rises above the lowest col linking it to higher ground. Sight lines allow for the Earth's curvature and normal atmospheric refraction, with someone 1.7 m tall standing at each end. Trees, buildings and haze aren't included, so real views can be more blocked.

      Link looks for high ground within walking distance of each of you, then checks every pair of spots. Radio needs more room than sight: for a strong signal the line should clear 60% of the first Fresnel zone, the football-shaped space around it. At 446 MHz over 8 km that's about 22 m at the midpoint. Radio uses the standard 4/3 Earth radius for bending. Clear means the full zone is free; sight line only means you can see each other but the zone is partly blocked, so radio may be weak. 2 m radio often works with less clearance than this. Walking distances are as the crow flies, so check paths and access on the Topo map before setting off, and do a test call.
